## Verdict

The direction is right and the conceptual core is sound — the four-way separation of dependency / contention / affinity / overlap, the evidence-precedence ladder, supersede-don't-mutate provenance, bounded portfolio windows, explainability as a requirement, and telemetry-before-learning are all correct and consistent with how tactus already thinks. The staging instinct (don't block the first parallel scheduler on this) is also right.

The document's central weakness is that **it is written one abstraction level above the tactus that actually exists, and doesn't engage with the machinery that was decided this week**. Roughly a third of its "foundations" are already built or decided; several of its mechanisms (execution intents, same-session affinity, a separate portfolio state store) conflict with load-bearing tactus invariants and should be replaced with mechanisms tactus already owns; and it has no cost model in a project whose design documents are unusually disciplined about measured economics. It needs a grounding pass before it can become a decision record.

## Status correction — 2026-08-13 Sol max review

This critique is retained as the fixed Fable 5 review artifact, so the original wording below is not silently rewritten. Finding 1 used "exists" too broadly: several mechanisms were architecturally **decided** in DESIGN/decision records but were not present in the reviewed Rust implementation. Accordingly, "keep recording" in the original sequencing recommendation means: first implement the decided task identity, worktree/candidate, lease, and merge telemetry; then **begin** recording predicted-versus-actual path deltas, stale re-review rate, and repair spend. Add `story_id` only after that identity substrate is real. The architectural recommendation to consume one substrate rather than build a competing portfolio system stands; its implementation-status premise is corrected here.

## What it gets right (keep all of this)

- **Contention ≠ dependency** (§7.2, §10) as distinct edge types with different scheduler semantics — correct, and the single most important modeling decision in the doc.
- **Evidence precedence with provenance** (§13–14): later evidence supersedes, never silently mutates. This is exactly the event-log philosophy.
- **Runtime changes timing, never product intent** (§7.4, §50) and the whole non-goals list (§5). This is the guardrail that keeps the coordinator from becoming a hidden project manager, and the doc states it well.
- **Conservative initial policy** (§80): overlap recommend-only, contention enforced only at high confidence, learning parked. Matches the design-council decision's manual-first posture.
- **"Unknown is not safe"** (§53) and progress-aware conflict response (§29).
- **Prediction-vs-outcome telemetry from day one** (§61, §63) — the same discipline as the predict-before-running protocol and DESIGN §23.2's calibration test, applied to a new prediction class.

## Finding 1 — The proposal doesn't know what already exists

Mapping its foundations (§69–71) and backlog (§74) onto the repo:

- **Deterministic path contention exists and is decided.** v0.2's dispatch leases (predicted, from `path_hints`) and actual-path queue leases *are* the proposal's §30 deterministic contention layer, including the prediction→actual replacement the proposal presents as a v0.5 novelty (§34). PORTFOLIO-003/-007/-016 are substantially the merge-queue decision's §6.
- **Integration ground truth exists and is decided.** The merge queue, stale re-verification, `already_present` disposition, repair lineage, and `merge_prepared.satisfies` cover §36 and §42 almost entirely. The decision even already mandates recording second-pass rate and cost — which is PORTFOLIO-017.
- **Stable identity exists.** Engine-issued numeric task keys (merge-queue decision §1) plus `external_ref` cover most of PORTFOLIO-001/-002; what's genuinely missing is a story identity above the task.
- **Footprints half-exist.** `path_hints` + actual changed paths are the predicted/observed footprint pair. What the proposal adds — symbols, invariants, schemas — is the speculative part.
- **Affinity exists.** The binder's affinity gradient, streak batching, and measured-switch-cost plan (DESIGN §10) are the proposal's §11 in more honest form (see Finding 5).
- **Hold/release semantics exist.** Park/defer already release the pipeline permit and predicted lease and re-dispatch under a new generation at the then-current head. A contention hold should *be* a `Deferred` with a recorded reason — not a new lifecycle. PORTFOLIO-015 is mostly done.

This is good news for the proposal — its ground-truth layer is real — but the document should be rewritten to *consume* these decisions rather than respecify them generically. As written, an implementer could reasonably build a second, parallel contention system.

Also missing entirely: the **two-phase lifecycle**. Tactus already has a "story designer" — it's Phase 1, and it already emits exactly what §16 asks for (DAG, acceptance, path hints, conventions brief, decisions record). Portfolio reconciliation (§17–20) is naturally a *Phase-1 activity over N frozen plans*, i.e., a design-council-shaped review pass, not engine machinery. That reframing moves the highest-value piece of this proposal (dedupe, shared prerequisites, cross-story deps) from "v0.5 engine feature" to "prompt product you could trial in v0.3 with zero engine changes" — the same manual-first path the council decision took.

## Finding 2 — The architecture question the proposal never answers: what *is* a portfolio?

Tactus invariants: one run, one `events.jsonl`, one coordinator, one fold; live state and replay are the same computation. The proposal's `PortfolioState` (§46), portfolio events (§45), and recovery section (§47–48) silently imply a second state store above multiple runs — two sources of truth, cross-log causality, and a recovery story that re-opens everything the merge-queue decision just closed.

**Recommendation: a portfolio is a run.** The plan is a forest of story-local DAGs plus cross-story edges; a story is a named sub-plan with its own acceptance criteria and requirement links; the integration head, merge queue, crash tables, and fold are inherited unchanged. Portfolio events become ordinary schema-4 events (fresh runs only, per the established no-in-flight-upgrade posture). Rolling story admission into a live portfolio — the one thing that strains frozen-plan-per-run — already has a precedent: `merge_rejected` registers a complete frozen task mid-run via one append. A frozen *story* payload admitted by event is the same mechanism one level up. Start with bounded release-train portfolios (matching §7.6) and grow to event-admitted stories later. This single decision collapses §45–49 to a page and makes §75.11 (holds survive restart) automatic.

Two smaller consequences: shared tasks (§18, §44) need no new state machine — they're ordinary tasks with in-edges from several stories plus requirement-link metadata, and `merge_prepared.satisfies` already demonstrates one commit settling multiple tasks. And waves (§21) should be a derived view like `Ready`/`Running`, never a stored artifact.

## Finding 3 — Execution intents violate the spirit of invariant 3; the engine has better evidence for free

"Ground truth is the diff, not the transcript." An `ExecutionIntent` (§24–27) is the transcript's *forecast* of a diff: self-reported, token-costing, and gameable — §77.10 concedes the gaming risk and answers it with exactly the machinery tactus already refuses to trust.

The engine owns every worktree. It can read `git status --porcelain` / `git diff --name-only` in any live worktree between scheduler turns — **observed mid-flight touched paths, continuously, at zero model spend, with no cooperation from the agent**. That strictly dominates self-reported expected paths for file-level contention. The honest caveat: observation lags intent (a worker may touch its riskiest file last), and a forecast has value before writes begin. But tactus already has the pre-write forecast — it's `path_hints`, authored by the designer at plan time. What §25's heavyweight intent schema adds over (designer hints → observed worktree paths → candidate actual paths) is mid-flight *semantic* self-description, which belongs in the deferred semantic layer anyway.

Two additions the proposal genuinely needs:

1. **A speculative-merge evidence tier.** Between "actual changed paths" and "integration result" in §14, insert `git merge-tree`: once two candidates (or a candidate and a live worktree diff) exist, "would these textually conflict" is answerable deterministically for CPU pennies, at hunk granularity. This is the missing middle tier — cheaper than semantic review, far more precise than path overlap.
2. **A granularity answer for small repos — including this one.** Tactus is ~25 source files and half of any plausible plan touches `engine.rs`, `lib.rs`, or `Cargo.toml`. File-level contention in a small crate ≈ total serialization, which means the proposal's deterministic layer would erase the parallelism it exists to protect (§77.1 worries about this without naming the cause). The design needs path classes — exclusive / hunk-mergeable / union-mergeable (lockfiles, `mod` lists, generated files) — and should lean on the merge-tree tier where file overlap fires. That also answers its own open question Q20.

## Finding 4 — There is no cost model, and in this project that's disqualifying

Measured reality the proposal must be written against: review is 44–77% of spend in cheap-worker runs; attempt count dominates cost; a stale candidate pays a **second full review** at integration (decided, deliberately); a repair task costs roughly a full task ($3–12); and the same reviewer has flip-flopped on the same line across two runs — the noise floor for exactly the kind of judgment §31's semantic contention analysis asks for.

Consequences:

- **The coordinator's objective has units.** Coordination earns `avoided (stale re-reviews + repairs + discarded candidates) − (reconciliation model spend + serialization delay)`. Every term is in the ledger or export-decisions already. §61's telemetry should be denominated in dollars, not counts. Rough arithmetic: a clustered reconciliation pass over ~30 tasks might cost a few dollars of model comparisons; it pays for itself iff it prevents ~1–2 repairs or a handful of stale re-reviews. That's plausible, not obvious — which is precisely why the telemetry must decide, and why semantic runtime coordination should ship *behind a measured trigger* (e.g., "stale-re-review + repair spend exceeded X% of run spend across N runs"), not by default.
- **Admission control is a P9 feature wearing a scheduling costume.** §22 lists "agent capacity" generically and §67 gestures at saturation, but capacity-aware admission is where this proposal touches tactus's actual wedge: admit the frontier-heavy story when spend-down says the window will otherwise expire; admit the docs story under conserve; throttle admission on *review* headroom, because review is the measured bottleneck and every additional parallel candidate raises the stale-re-review probability. This synergy is absent from the proposal and it's the most tactus-native thing about the whole feature.
- **Holds have a seat cost.** Holding a task mid-flight after inspect either kills a warm context (re-load cost) or idles a subscription seat the capacity engine is trying to drain. §28/§29 need a policy line about what Hold means for an already-running session; at 2–3-way personal parallelism the answer is probably "holds apply only before dispatch," which deletes most of the live-coordination machinery for v0.5.

## Finding 5 — Same-session affinity is architecturally hostile here

§11/§41's "assign to the same worker session / resumed session" collides with three deliberate tactus decisions: fresh-context-per-stage (DESIGN §22, adopted with credit), worktree-per-generation (a resumed session's beliefs are tied to one tree; session ids deliberately don't survive replay for exactly this reason), and the candidate/generation model. What's actually available — and already designed — is the affinity *gradient*: same model + pool within the provider's cache window, streak batching, sequential dispatch of affine tasks on one binding. The proposal's affinity clusters reduce to a plan-time hint feeding the existing binder tie-break. Keep the cluster concept, delete "same session," and the reviewer-independence worry (§41, Q22) mostly evaporates.

## Finding 6 — What the proposal doesn't coordinate but should

Two cross-story surfaces are missing, and both are more frequently exercised than write conflicts at this project's scale:

- **Artifacts.** Each story's design phase produced its own conventions brief and decisions record. Two briefs can disagree (error-handling idiom, naming, module conventions) — which produces exactly the "architectural inconsistency" §2 fears, through a channel no path or symbol analysis sees. Portfolio reconciliation should reconcile *artifacts* first: one merged conventions brief, decisions-record contradictions surfaced as coordination questions. Cheap, design-time, high yield. Relatedly, acceptance-criteria *text* across stories is where semantic conflict is cheapest to detect — two stories asserting different expectations about the same behavior is visible at plan time without any runtime intent machinery.
- **Questions.** P8 is a pillar and the proposal never mentions it. A question raised by a shared task parks work in several stories; the architect pre-filter now has N decisions records to search; and "story B's worker just hit the ambiguity story A resolved yesterday" is a cross-story dedup the coordinator should own. This belongs in §23's runtime loop.

## Section-level notes

- **§10 contention is symmetric; every resolution is directional.** "Allow A2, hold B1" (§26) picks a direction by "began first" — ad hoc. The direction policy (progress, priority, downstream value) must be part of the model and the recorded explanation, or add a directed `Sequence` edge kind distinct from symmetric contention (this also cleans up §20's "architectural sequencing preference," which is currently shoehorned).
- **§43(6) story completion is a floating condition.** "Present on the current authoritative integration head" makes completion non-monotonic. Pin completion to the integration SHA at which acceptance passed, per the `satisfies` precedent.
- **§25 `discovered_conflicts` implies workers see each other's tasks.** That's a real design choice (context leakage, gaming §77.10) the doc makes accidentally. Coordinator-side comparison only; drop the field.
- **§72 initial scope is 2–3× too big.** Fourteen items including intents, live contention updates, and semantic review is several months of work stacked on an unproven premise.
- **§52 clustering** is over-engineered at the configured bounds (≤32 tasks = ≤496 pairs); fine to keep for later scale, not a v0.5 requirement.
- **Worktree economics:** the merge-queue decision lists "worktrees and duplicate build artifacts are cheap enough for 2–3-way" as an *assumption*; portfolio scale multiplies it (each Rust worktree gets its own `target/`), on WSL. Needs a line.
- **Naming:** DESIGN §1 already uses "portfolio" for the *capacity* portfolio ("treats your Claude Max windows … as one portfolio"). Two portfolios in one product's vocabulary will hurt; consider "program" or "release train" for the work-side concept.

## Answers to the open questions worth answering now (§78)

- **Q1** Event stream, primary — forced by portfolio-as-run; planning views are folds.
- **Q2** Shared tasks belong to multiple stories via requirement links; no synthetic story (it would need fake acceptance semantics).
- **Q4** Footprints are versioned evidence records, not mutable task fields — §33's own provenance requirement already implies this.
- **Q5** Plan revisions are frozen-spawn/supersede events in the log, the mechanism merge repairs already use — never a mutated plan object.
- **Q11/Q12** No task publishes an intent by default; the engine observes worktrees. A pre-write forecast already exists as `path_hints`.
- **Q18** Advisory by default; only deterministic high-confidence evidence enforces. (§80 already says this — promote it from "recommended policy" to the design.)
- **Q21/Q23** Same session: no (Finding 5). Affinity never combines tasks — combining is overlap's job, and overlap is manual.
- **Q26** Admission automatic within bounds, but always previewed — this is a `--dry-run` culture; `tactus portfolio plan` should be the dry-run.
- **Q29** Baseline = v0.2 naive (leases + merge queue, no reconciliation), measured in accepted tasks/hour and $/accepted task *including* re-review and repair spend. Export-decisions already carries the rows.

## Recommended sequencing (replacing §72/§79)

**Now (inside v0.2, nearly free):** add optional `story_id` to `Task` alongside `external_ref`; keep recording predicted-vs-actual path deltas, stale-re-review rate, and repair rate in dollars (the merge-queue decision already mandates most of this). That's the entire foundation the proposal actually needs preserved early.

**v0.3-era, zero engine changes:** trial portfolio reconciliation as a design-phase prompt product — run a council-style pass over two or three real frozen plans; measure whether it finds real overlap, missing cross-deps, and conventions conflicts. This is the proposal's highest value-per-dollar component and it needs no code.

**v0.4-ish — multi-story runs:** forest plans, cross-story dependency edges, per-story acceptance, story-scoped status; admission = the human choosing what goes in the plan. (Proposal §71, and honestly most of §75.)

**v0.5 — the earned engine features:** observed mid-flight worktree paths; the merge-tree evidence tier; path classes for small-repo granularity; capacity-aware admission (P9-coupled); dollar-denominated prediction telemetry. **Semantic runtime coordination, intents, and live holds ship only if the recorded stale/repair spend crosses a pre-registered threshold** — the proposal's own §36 philosophy, applied to itself.

---

**Bottom line:** adopt the relational vocabulary, the evidence-precedence ladder, and the non-goals verbatim; reframe reconciliation as a Phase-1 activity over frozen plans; make "portfolio = a run" explicit and inherit the merge-queue decision's machinery instead of respecifying it; replace self-reported intents with engine observation plus speculative merges; wire admission into the capacity engine; and cut the initial scope to the multi-story substrate plus design-time reconciliation, with runtime semantic coordination gated on measured repair economics. With that pass, this becomes a strong v0.5 target document rather than a parallel design that would compete with its own engine.

# Blind normalized design council

**Status:** Draft
**Review:** Unreviewed
**Target:** v0.3+ (manual protocol now; product machinery after critical parallelism)
**Filed:** 2026-08-15
**Origin:** owner prompts defining a Fable-design, Sol-challenge, Opus-implementation workflow, 2026-08-15.

---

## Summary

Design generation, design challenge, synthesis, implementation, and code review
should be separate seats. Before a challenger sees another model's design,
Tactus normalizes the artifact and removes provider/model/style identity. This
reduces status and brand bias while preserving assumptions, invariants,
trade-offs, risks, and acceptance criteria.

The protocol is manual until the design-pane/council work is earned. Durable
product policy names capabilities and roles, not today's vendor roster.

## Current manual protocol

The current preferred experiment is:

1. an anonymous design generator produces a specific architecture;
2. a normalizer converts it to the council schema and strips seat identity;
3. an anonymous frontier challenger attacks assumptions, omissions, and unsafe
   coupling without seeing the generator's identity;
4. the owner or a named synthesis seat resolves the critique and records
   accepted/rejected changes;
5. a strong implementation seat executes the frozen design;
6. independent code review follows the bounded review-governance process.

Operationally, the owner currently prefers Fable 5 for initial design, Sol 5.6
Max for design challenge, Opus 5 for implementation, and Sol for code review.
Those bindings are measured configuration, not normative architecture. A later
model can occupy a seat when it satisfies the capability and independence rules.

## Normalized design envelope

Every generator is asked for the same content and the normalizer emits one
canonical envelope:

- problem and success statement;
- known facts and explicit assumptions;
- hard invariants and threat model;
- proposed architecture and ownership boundaries;
- alternatives considered;
- trade-offs and rejected choices;
- failure, recovery, and migration behavior;
- acceptance criteria and proof strategy;
- unresolved questions and confidence limits;
- content-addressed supporting evidence.

The envelope removes model/provider names, self-references, stylistic headers,
marketing language, token metadata, and other source hints. It preserves exact
technical claims and labels any lossy normalization.

## Blindness and independence

A seat does not learn the identity, raw prose, or verdict of another seat before
its own artifact settles. The challenger receives the normalized proposal plus
the same repository/design context, not the generator transcript.

Blindness reduces model-name and rhetoric bias. It cannot remove shared prompt,
training-data, or repository-context bias, so disagreement and convergence are
evidence rather than truth. After settlement, the audit record may reveal seat
identity, model, family, effort, CLI version, and normalization version.

The synthesis record maps every material challenge to accepted, rejected,
deferred, duplicate, or needs-human with a reason. It never silently produces a
third design with no trace to either input.

## Relationship to the adopted council decision

This proposal extends the
[manual-first council decision](../decisions/2026-08-11-design-council.md). It
does not restore the rejected analytics-heavy council machinery. The experiment
arm remains first: use multiple families on a small number of difficult designs,
record whether independent generation/challenge finds material issues, then
decide which automation is justified.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Two differently styled source designs normalize into the same schema without
   exposing provider/model identity.
2. Technical assertions, uncertainty, alternatives, and acceptance criteria
   survive normalization or are flagged as lossy.
3. A challenger cannot inspect the generator identity or previous verdict
   before settling its own critique.
4. The synthesis record dispositions every material critique.
5. Post-settlement audit reveals the real seat/model/configuration without
   changing what later seats originally saw.
6. Manual operation requires no new engine feature and cannot block the initial
   parallel scheduler.
7. Product automation is gated by measured gains over a single strong design
   plus independent challenge.

## Non-goals

- majority vote or automatic consensus;
- hiding identity from the final audit record;
- pretending normalization removes all cognitive bias;
- fixed permanent assignments to current model brands;
- building council analytics before the experiment proves value;
- coupling implementation parallelism to council automation.

## Open questions for council

1. Should synthesis be a model seat, the owner, or both in a two-step process?
2. Which fields may normalization safely reorder or compress?
3. How is accidental identity leakage detected and recorded?
4. When does independent generation add more value than a single design plus a
   strong adversarial challenge?

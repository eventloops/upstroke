# Proposal disposition ledger and rationale history

**Status:** Draft
**Review:** Unreviewed
**Target:** v0.3+ (repository convention first; product projection later)
**Filed:** 2026-08-15
**Origin:** owner prompt to preserve why proposals were approved or rejected for future teams and customers, 2026-08-15.

---

## Summary

Tactus should retain the reasoning behind accepted, rejected, parked,
withdrawn, and superseded proposals. A decision record preserves the winning
authority; the proposal ledger preserves the alternatives, objections, evidence,
and revisit conditions that explain how the project arrived there.

The first version is a repository convention over Markdown and links. A later
event-backed projection may expose the same history in product documentation.

## Why this is distinct from DESIGN

DESIGN is the current living authority. It should be concise enough to implement
and should not accumulate every abandoned path. Decision records explain what
was adopted at a point in time. Neither alone makes rejected ideas easy to find
or tells a future contributor whether an apparently new suggestion was already
considered under different evidence.

The ledger is historical evidence, not authority. If it conflicts with current
DESIGN, DESIGN wins and the inconsistency must be repaired.

## Stable lifecycle

Every proposal has a stable proposal identity and append-only disposition
entries. A transition records:

- prior and new state;
- date and actor/seat;
- concise reason;
- evidence and critiques considered;
- alternatives and trade-offs;
- resulting decision/DESIGN/implementation links;
- revisit condition or `none`;
- predecessor, successor, supersedes, and reconsidered-by links where relevant.

Initial states use the existing proposal vocabulary: Draft, In council,
Decided, Parked, Withdrawn. The ledger additionally distinguishes a council
outcome such as accepted, rejected, needs-human, or superseded without rewriting
the original proposal.

A rejected proposal remains searchable. It may re-enter council only through a
new disposition citing materially new evidence or a previously recorded revisit
condition; it cannot silently be copied into a fresh file and treated as novel.

## Review evidence state

Proposal status and review state are orthogonal. A newly filed proposal begins:

```text
Status: Draft
Review: Unreviewed
```

Review may move through In review and Reviewed with links to immutable critique
artifacts. A status transition does not imply review happened, and a critique
does not make a proposal authoritative.

## Customer-facing projection

A curated documentation page may later answer questions such as:

- why does Tactus use one integration queue?
- why was a different retry policy rejected?
- which limitation is temporary versus deliberate?
- what evidence would cause the project to reconsider?

The projection links to source records but omits private run material, model
transcripts, secrets, and internal security details that would create risk. It
is explanatory documentation, not an automatically published dump.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Every status change for a sample proposal is chronologically reconstructible.
2. Accepted and rejected alternatives retain their evidence and rationale.
3. Supersession is navigable in both directions.
4. A repeated proposal is detected through stable identity, aliases, or a
   required related-work search.
5. Current authority remains unambiguous: proposal and ledger records cannot
   override DESIGN or an active decision.
6. The public projection excludes private evidence by default.
7. Repository-only operation works before any event schema or UI exists.

## Sequencing

Use explicit `Review: Unreviewed` metadata for the current batch now. Design and
review the complete lifecycle after the critical parallel scheduler is working.
Do not make ledger automation a prerequisite for that scheduler.

## Non-goals

- preserving private model transcripts in the repository;
- automatically deciding which proposal wins;
- turning DESIGN into a changelog;
- preventing an administrator from rewriting Git history;
- building a customer documentation portal in the first change.
